Overview
Key observations across the full online store funnel

Mary Frances operates a Shopify storefront selling designer beaded and embellished handbags and accessories — a specialty product with a passionate collector audience and an average order value of $244. Despite nearly one million online store sessions over nine months, the site is currently converting at approximately 0.46%, which is below the 1.5–2.5% range typically observed for specialty fashion eCommerce. This audit identifies four primary areas where improvements could potentially lift conversion performance: a checkout price-discovery gap, an underperforming Facebook/Instagram channel mix, an under-exploited Remarkety email programme, and high cart abandonment with limited on-page friction reduction. A data anomaly was observed in March–April 2026 and is noted here for context.

Data Note — March & April 2026

An ATC event double-firing was observed in March 2026 (ATC rate: 6.07%) and April 2026 (5.75%) — approximately 3–4× the monthly average and driven almost entirely by the direct channel. This pattern inflated add-to-cart and checkout figures for those two months without a corresponding lift in completed purchases. March and April figures should be treated with caution in any historical trend analysis, and any automated bidding campaigns (Google Smart Bidding, Meta Advantage+) that were running during this period may benefit from a performance data review.

04
Key Findings & Recommendations
Four key observations with data context and suggested improvements
🛒
Observation 1 — Checkout abandonment may be significantly impacting direct traffic value

Direct traffic converts at only 0.49% session-to-purchase — but its ATC-to-checkout rate is an exceptional 93.3%. These visitors appear highly motivated. However, approximately 89.9% do not complete a purchase after reaching checkout. A likely contributing factor, observed during a review of the live checkout, is a price-discovery gap: customers see $195.00 on the cart page, then encounter $14.95 shipping + $17.32 tax at checkout — a 16.5% cost increase at the most committed stage of the journey.

Suggested improvement: Surfacing an estimated shipping and tax figure on the cart page could potentially reduce this gap. Ensuring express checkout options (Shop Pay, PayPal, Google Pay — already present) are visually prominent may also help. Additionally, removing the pre-checked marketing email opt-in and the Company field from the delivery form could reduce unnecessary friction.

No return policy reminder, no secure payment indicators, no delivery estimate visible at cart stage. A brief reassurance line could potentially reduce hesitation on $200+ purchases.

Gift buyers are a core Mary Frances segment. A "This is a gift" prompt could potentially increase purchase commitment and differentiate from generic retailers.

💸
Observation 2 — Facebook drives 39.7% of traffic but appears to contribute only 9.8% of estimated revenue

Facebook is the single largest traffic source with 387,484 sessions — yet it converts at just 0.12%, generating only $285 per 1,000 sessions. Compare this to Google at $2,306/1k sessions, or Remarkety at $4,971/1k sessions. Every 1,000 Facebook visitors generates an estimated fraction of what Remarkety visitors generate in revenue. The audience coming from Facebook appears to be in discovery mode — consistent with novelty, giftable bags that are commonly saved and shared on social but less frequently purchased via a direct ad click.

Suggested improvement: Reallocating Meta spend away from purchase-objective campaigns toward traffic/awareness and retargeting audiences could potentially improve the revenue contribution from this channel. Retargeting visitors who added to cart but did not checkout may represent a higher-intent Facebook audience worth prioritising.

✉
Observation 3 — Remarkety shows strong efficiency indicators and may be under-exploited

Remarkety (email) converts at 1.93% with an AOV of $258 — the second-most-efficient channel in the entire mix. From just 3.8% of total sessions it generates 16.1% of all online store revenue. Each Remarkety session generates an estimated $4,971 per 1,000 sessions, compared to an estimated $285 per 1,000 for Facebook. Yet Remarkety session volumes are modest and relatively flat across the 9-month period, suggesting the email programme may be limited largely to abandoned cart recovery.

Recommended additional flows:

  • Post-purchase cross-sell — 7 days after purchase, featuring thematically related products (cat bag → music, travel, or novelty bags)
  • Win-back sequence — triggered at 60, 90, 120 days of inactivity; lead with "New collection" angle (collectors want to know what dropped)
  • VIP early access — for repeat customers, first-look at new arrivals (leverages the collector psychology of the core audience)
  • Browse abandonment — triggered after 3+ product views without an add-to-cart action, featuring the most-viewed items
🛒
Observation 4 — 64.1% cart abandonment with limited on-page friction reduction

Across all channels, 64.1% of visitors who add to cart never reach checkout. The abandoned cart email via Remarkety recovers a portion of these, but the primary drop-off appears to occur before any email triggers. For Mary Frances's customer base — collectors, gift buyers, special occasion shoppers — the core friction is likely price hesitation and browsing intent rather than a broken UX.

Recommended cart-stage additions:

  • Free-shipping progress bar — "You're $X away from free shipping" with a threshold of $200–$225. At an AOV of $244 most customers are already near this; the bar makes it visible and incentivises action. Display theme-matched small accessories (charms, coin purses) as suggested add-ons.
  • Exit-intent popup — triggered on cart exit with a non-empty cart. Offer a low-friction value add (free gift message, save-my-cart link) rather than a discount, which trains customers to abandon for deals.
  • Return policy reminder — "Free returns · Secure checkout · Ships in 2–3 days" below the Checkout button (the policy exists; it just needs visibility at the decision moment)
  • Sticky Checkout CTA on mobile — fixed bottom bar with total + Checkout button so the CTA is always visible without scrolling
